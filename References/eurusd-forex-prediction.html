The Prediction Challenge

The foreign exchange market processes roughly $5 trillion in daily volume with high volatility. Only 2% of traders successfully predict directional movement—the market responds to economic indicators, geopolitical events, central bank decisions, and human behavior simultaneously.

Predicting daily direction (binary classification: up or down) differs from forecasting exact price movements. The approach treats currency pairs as short-term directional bets rather than long-term value forecasting.

Experimental Design

The study spans April 2013 to December 2022, with data from 2013-2019 for training, 2020-2021 for validation, and 2022 for final evaluation. Economic indicators from the USA and European Union were combined with market indices (DAX, S&P500, NASDAQ) and 13 foreign exchange pairs to capture cross-market dynamics.

Key limitation: Economic indicators are used at midnight valuation, whereas real releases occur throughout the trading day. This limits the model's ability to capture volatility spikes around announcement times.

Problem Setup

Target Definition

The binary target is defined simply: if tomorrow's EUR/USD close price exceeds today's close, the label is 1 (up). Otherwise, 0 (down).

Direction(t) = 1 if close_price(t+1) > close_price(t)
            = 0 otherwise

This one-day-ahead prediction creates a straightforward binary classification problem. A 50% baseline represents random guessing.

Trading Mechanics: Contract for Difference

The strategy assumes trading via CFDs (Contracts for Difference), where profit depends on predicting direction correctly—not the magnitude of price movement.

A correct directional prediction on a large move is more profitable than a correct prediction on a small move. This means accuracy alone does not determine profit; the distribution of correct/incorrect predictions across high-volatility and low-volatility periods matters significantly.

Critical insight: A model with 55% accuracy may outperform one with 57% accuracy if the 55% model captures the larger price swings correctly while the 57% model succeeds mostly on small, low-profit moves.

Evaluation Metrics

Two metrics are tracked:

Metric Definition Interpretation
Accuracy Fraction of correct directional predictions How often the model guesses the direction correctly
Profit Product of cumulative price ratios, weighted by prediction Annualized return if following the strategy live

Profit is computed as:

P(t) = ∏[1 if pred(i)=1 then C(i)/C(i-1)
          else C(i-1)/C(i)]

This formula captures buy-and-hold over 1 day, either long (if predicted up) or short (if predicted down).

Three primary data sources were combined:

Three Dataset Representations

Daily Features Only

Current day's OHLCV for all market indices and forex pairs, plus current economic indicators. Minimal temporal context.

Advantage: Low feature dimensionality, fast training.

Disadvantage: Ignores short-term momentum and trending patterns.

Daily + Historical (90-day lookback)

Current day plus preceding 90 days of OHLCV data. Captures short-term trends and seasonal patterns at a rolling 3-month window.

Advantage: Captures momentum and mean-reversion patterns.

Disadvantage: Feature explosion; requires careful regularization.

Technical Indicators

Current day + 18 technical indicators derived from historical prices: moving averages (SMA, WMA), momentum oscillators (RSI, Stochastic), trend indicators (MACD), and volatility measures.

Advantage: Domain-engineered features; captures market microstructure patterns traders use manually.

Disadvantage: Hand-crafted features may overfit to specific market regimes.

Result: Achieved highest accuracy across all model types.

Date Encoding

Temporal information is encoded differently by algorithm family:

Model Type Encoding
Tree-based (RF, XGB, etc.) Ordinal: day (1-31), month (1-12), weekday (0-6)
Distance-based (SVM, KNN) Cyclic: sin/cos for day, month, weekday

Sine/cosine encoding ensures that day 31 and day 1 are close in feature space (cyclicity), important for non-tree models.

Machine Learning Algorithms

21 distinct algorithms were evaluated, spanning linear models, distance-based, tree-based, and ensemble methods.

Algorithm Families

Logistic Regression

Baseline linear classifier. Learned a linear decision boundary in feature space.

Result on dataset 2: 51.1% accuracy, 8.0% annual profit. Simple baseline; outperformed by complex models.

K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)

Instance-based learner: classifies each sample by majority vote of its K nearest neighbors. Sensitive to feature scaling and dimensionality.

Support Vector Machine (SVM)

Margin-maximizing hyperplane classifier. Tested with 4 kernels: linear, RBF (radial basis function), sigmoid, polynomial. RBF kernel did not generalize well (negative profit on multiple datasets), suggesting severe overfitting to training distribution.

Decision Tree

Recursive binary partitioning. Prone to overfitting without pruning, but allows feature importance inspection.

Bagging (Bootstrap Aggregating)

Trains multiple models on random subsamples, averages predictions. Reduces variance. Applied to Decision Trees, Logistic Regression, KNN, and SVM variants.

Gradient Boosting

Sequential ensemble: each tree corrects residuals of the previous. Includes Histogram Gradient Boosting, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost.

Random Forests & Extra Trees

Parallel ensemble: average predictions from many deep trees trained on random subsets. Extra Trees adds randomness to split thresholds, often faster.

Algorithm Scaling

Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

Each algorithm was trained twice: once on raw features, once after PCA decorrelation (without dimension reduction).

Surprising finding: PCA harmed performance across the board. Highest PCA accuracy was 54.98% vs. 58.52% without PCA. Hypothesis: PCA linearizes the feature space, breaking temporal relationships encoded in raw features.

This aligns with findings in other financial time-series work that non-linear feature relationships matter more than variance reduction.

Meta-Estimators & Stacking

What Are Meta-Estimators?

A meta-estimator (or stacked model) trains a second-level model using predictions from multiple base models as features.

Architecture

The experiment used a three-stage stacking approach:

1
Stage 1: Train 3 base models on different datasets
2
Collect predictions from all 3
3
Meta-learner trained on (original features + base predictions)

Results on Meta-Estimators

Metric Base Models Meta-Estimators
Max Accuracy 58.52% 57.23%
Best Profit 29.54% 32.48%
Key observation: Meta-estimators slightly reduced accuracy but improved profit. The decision tree meta-estimator on dataset 3 combined base predictions to select large profitable moves even when base accuracy was moderate.

Why Stacking Helps Profit, Not Accuracy

Base models on different feature sets capture different patterns. By learning how to weight their predictions, the meta-learner skips low-value trades (small price moves) and concentrates on high-variance scenarios. This trades slightly lower hit rate for higher profit per trade.

Preprocessing & Hyperparameter Tuning

Cross-Validation Strategy

Standard k-fold cross-validation biases financial forecasting by mixing past and future data. Instead, rolling-window (walk-forward) validation was used:

This prevents data leakage: the model never sees future information when making predictions.

Bayesian Hyperparameter Optimization

Three sequential Bayesian searches were used, each informing the next:

Search Focus Output
Search 1 Hyperparameters on full feature set Good hyperparameters + full feature mask
Search 2 Hyperparameters + coarse feature selection Refined hyperparameters + pruned features
Search 3 Hyperparameters + fine feature selection Final model + optimal feature subset

This cascading approach accelerates convergence: the third search benefits from good starting points, exploring a massive space more efficiently.

Technique note: Knowledge transfer between sequential optimizers is non-trivial. Initial guesses matter enormously in high-dimensional spaces.

Optimization Criterion

Accuracy was the criterion for all hyperparameter tuning and feature selection. This assumes accuracy correlates with profit—which the study found to be imperfect, but provides a stable objective for optimization.

Key Insights

1. Feature Engineering Dominates

Finding: Dataset 3 (technical indicators) outperformed raw features by 2-3% accuracy across all algorithms.

Implication: In financial prediction, domain knowledge matters more than model complexity. Traders' manually-crafted indicators (RSI, MACD, moving averages) encode market microstructure that models can leverage.

Actionable: Feature engineering should incorporate domain expertise first; algorithmic feature selection comes second.

2. PCA Harms Time-Series Prediction

Finding: Decorrelating features via PCA reduced accuracy from 58.52% to 54.98%.

Hypothesis: PCA linearizes the feature space, destroying temporal dependencies. Forex prices have non-linear momentum and mean-reversion cycles that linear transformations obscure.

Implication: Dimensionality reduction via variance-based methods may not suit financial time series. Non-linear manifold learning or temporal autoencoders could be better.

3. Accuracy ≠ Profit

Finding: A 55% accurate model outperformed a 57% accurate model in profit.

Root cause: The 55% model captured high-volatility moves (large swings) more often, while the 57% model won many small trades.

Implication: Optimizing solely for accuracy leads to suboptimal profit. Trading systems should optimize for: (accuracy × expected move size).

4. Stacking Improves Profit, Not Accuracy

Finding: Meta-estimators achieved 57.23% accuracy (lower than base models' 58.52%) but 32.48% profit (higher than base models' 29.54%).

Explanation: The meta-learner learns to ignore low-confidence or low-profit predictions from base models. It becomes selective, trading fewer times but more profitably.

Implication: Stacking works for trading because it optimizes the right metric: profit per unit risk, not raw hit rate.

5. Economic Indicators Have Limited Predictive Power at Daily Horizon

Finding: Inclusion of GDP, CPI, interest rates did not dramatically improve accuracy beyond market indices alone.

Reason: Macro indicators are released irregularly and move markets in seconds when published. By midnight (when models make daily predictions), the shock has dissipated.

Implication: For daily predictions, market prices embed macro information faster than econometric models can exploit it. Higher-frequency (hourly/minute) prediction could better capture announcement effects.

6. Tree-Based Models Outperform Distance-Based

Finding: Gradient boosting (XGBoost, LightGBM, Histogram GB) and Random Forests consistently outperformed SVM and KNN.

Reason: Trees automatically detect non-linear interactions (e.g., RSI > 70 AND volatility > threshold = strong signal). Distance-based models struggle in high-dimensional spaces.

Implication: For feature-rich financial datasets, ensemble tree methods are the pragmatic choice.

Summary

Best practice recipe: Use domain-engineered features → train with ensemble tree methods → optimize for profit, not accuracy → combine via stacking for selective, high-conviction trades.

Limitations & Future Work

Current Limitations

Economic Indicators Timing

Economic indicators (GDP, CPI, interest rates) are used at midnight valuation, but they are actually released throughout the trading day. When a major indicator is released (e.g., U.S. jobs report at 8:30 AM), volatility spikes and moves happen within seconds.

Impact: Models cannot capture intraday shocks, limiting predictive power for daily direction on announcement days.

Fixed Prediction Horizon

The approach predicts exactly 1 day ahead. Very short-term traders (intraday) need minute-level predictions; longer-term investors need week/month horizons.

Gap: 1-day horizon may be suboptimal for both strategies.

No Slippage or Transaction Costs

Profit calculations assume perfect execution at the bid/ask close without slippage, commissions, or spreads.

Reality: In live trading, spreads (typically 1-2 pips for EUR/USD) and commissions would reduce 32.48% annual return by several percentage points.

Stationarity Assumption

The model assumes market statistics (mean, variance, correlations) are stable across 2013-2022. Central bank policy shifts, Brexit, pandemic events, and geopolitical shocks fundamentally alter market behavior.

Implication: Models trained on 2015-2019 data may not generalize to 2022 without retraining.

Future Work

Intraday Prediction

Refine predictions to hourly or minute resolution. Capture announcement-driven volatility spikes. Requires minute-level OHLCV data and real-time economic calendars.

Ensemble of Prediction Horizons

Train separate models for 1-day, 5-day, and 20-day ahead predictions. Combine them via meta-learner that selects the best-consensus prediction.

Attention-Based Architecture

Replace hand-crafted technical indicators with learned attention mechanisms (Transformers). Allow the model to discover which historical windows matter most dynamically.

Regime-Switching Models

Detect market regimes (trending, ranging, high volatility, low volatility) and train separate sub-models for each. Route predictions through the appropriate regime model.

Causal Feature Discovery

Instead of optimizing via Bayesian search, use causal inference (Causal Forest, SHAP) to identify which features causally influence EUR/USD direction, filtering out spurious correlations.

Multi-Task Learning

Jointly predict direction AND volatility, AND return magnitude. Shared representations may improve generalization beyond binary classification.

Conclusion

Takeaway: Binary direction prediction is solvable at 58%+ accuracy with off-the-shelf ML, but translating accuracy to profit requires careful attention to feature engineering, metric selection, and ensemble design. The gap between accuracy and trading returns highlights why most retail traders fail—optimizing the wrong objective.
