Key Findings

  • Dataset 2 (90-Day Lookback) is near-random (accuracy 0.48-0.56, AUC-ROC 0.48-0.58) for every one of the 12 models, not just the LR/LGBM smoke test from an earlier session. This confirms the p >> n risk flagged in References/DATASET_2_3_PLAN.md (~9,110 columns vs. ~2,700 training rows) is a persistent, project-wide problem rather than a fold-1 or model-specific artifact. Tree-based models (RF/XGB/CatBoost/etc.) do not handle the extreme column count any better than linear models here.
  • Dataset 3 (Technical Indicators) gives a clear, consistent improvement for tree/boosting-based models: Bagging_DT, CatBoost, ET, HGB, LGBM, RF and XGB all reach 0.80-0.85 CV accuracy and 0.87-0.92 AUC-ROC, versus 0.53-0.62 accuracy on Dataset 1 for the same models. This matches the paper's (Guyard & Deriaz 2024) finding that domain-knowledge technical features help gradient-boosting/tree ensembles specifically.
  • Linear models do not benefit from Dataset 3 — LR and Bagging_LR actually score lower on Dataset 3 (CV accuracy ~0.64) than on Dataset 1 (~0.74). Many collinear technical indicators (same window sizes 3/7/14/30/60/90 repeated across 12 indicator families) likely hurt an unregularised linear decision boundary more than they help it.
  • DT and KNN remain the weakest models across all three datasets — consistent with expectations for a single decision tree (high variance) and a distance-based method on high-dimensional, non-scaled financial features.
